El Salvador National Dish !

Las Pupusas :

What is the Pupusa ?

The pupusa is a thick corn or rice flour flatbread from El Salvador stuffed with fillings like cheese, beans, or meat and griddled until crisp.
It is the national dish of El Salvador, traditionally eaten by hand with a tangy cabbage slaw called curtido.

About the pupusas history & info !

The pupusa was an ancient food first made thousands of years ago by the Pipil people in El Salvador. The name comes from the indigenous Nahuat language. People shape the dough by hand, stuff the center, and flatten it into a round disc.
They cook the cakes on a hot, flat griddle called a comal. As they cook, the inside filling gets hot and melts.
A bit of cheese often oozes out and gets crispy and brown on the hot surface Pupusas are always served with curtido, a crunchy pickled cabbage slaw.
They also come with a mild red tomato salsa poured on top. This fresh, sour topping cuts through the rich, cheesy taste of the hot flatbread.

Ingredients to make Pupusas:

Dough Ingredients:Masa harina (corn flour)Warm water and Salt
Optional Fillings include: Quesillo or mozzarella cheese ,Refried black or pinto beans ,Chicharrón (seasoned, finely ground cooked pork),Loroco (edible green flower buds from Central America),Cooked squash or zucchiniToppings
Sides:Curtido (shredded cabbage, carrots, onions, vinegar, and oregano)Salsa roja (blended cooked tomato sauce)Vegetable oil or cooking oil (for the griddle)
