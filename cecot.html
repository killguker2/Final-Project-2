EL CECOT !

The Centro de Confinamiento del Terrorismo, universally known as CECOT, is a maximum-security mega-prison located in a rural, isolated area of Tecoluca, El Salvador. Opened in early 2023 by Salvadoran President Nayib Bukele,
it was custom-built to serve as the epicenter of his administration's massive, aggressive crackdown on gang violence.
Spanning a vast 23-hectare complex, it is widely recognized as one of the largest specialized detention facilities in the world, explicitly engineered to isolate high-ranking gang members and violent offenders.

Scale and Architecture :

The structure of CECOT is massive and heavily fortified, featuring eight sprawling pavilions designed to hold up to 40,000 inmates.
Surrounding the complex are reinforced concrete walls rising 11 meters high—built to withstand heavy physical attacks—alongside electric fencing, 19 distinct watchtowers, and multi-layered military checkpoints. Inside, prisoners are packed
tightly into communal cells holding 65 to 70 individuals, sleep on tiered metal bunks without mattresses, and share open, basic sanitation facilities under constant camera surveillance.

Severe Detention Conditions :

The facility prioritizes high-security isolation over inmate rehabilitation, enforcing a lifestyle of absolute confinement. Inmates are completely denied family visits, phone calls, and direct contact with the outside world, with legal proceedings taking place exclusively through mass virtual court hearings. Furthermore, inmates are never permitted outdoors for recreation or fresh air; instead, they spend almost their entire day locked in their communal cells or occasionally lined up in rows within the interior hallways under heavy armed guard supervision.

Human Rights and Geopolitical Spotlight :

CECOT has frequently sat at the center of intense international debate and scrutiny from global human rights organizations. Watchdogs like Human Rights Watch routinely condemn the facility for its crowded conditions, lack of due process, and alleged systemic mistreatment of detainees.
Despite the international outcry regarding civil liberties, the prison remains highly popular among the local Salvadoran populace for dramatically plunging the country's historic homicide rates,
and it has increasingly drawn interest from other Latin American leaders looking to replicate the high-security model.
